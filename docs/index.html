Teaching an AI to Land a Rocket in Gusty Wind

Fly a 2-D rocket with a PID controller or one of two reinforcement-learning pilots, then compare how often each one lands.

Moving a condition slider switches to Custom. Changing the conditions or seed resets the flight.

About / How to use

The rocket must touch down slowly (vertical speed at most 2 m/s, sideways at most 1 m/s), upright (tilt at most 10°) and within 10 m of the pad centre. Its engines are on/off only.

  1. Pick a controller and conditions, then press Start. Pause holds the flight; Reset goes back to the beginning.
  2. The same seed gives the same start and wind for every controller, so you can compare them fairly.
  3. Press Run batch to fly many landings with all three controllers and estimate success rates with 95% confidence intervals.
  • Calm: no wind. Training: wind up to 8 m/s with mass and gravity varied. Unseen: wind of 10–15 m/s with strong gusts, outside the training range.
  • Motion is integrated with fourth-order Runge–Kutta at 0.1 s; gusts follow an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.
  • The two DQN pilots are networks trained in the notebook (seed 0 of ten each). Results vary a lot between training seeds: in unseen wind the ten-seed averages are 76% (calm) and 75% (domain randomisation), against 92% for the PID.
  • All parameters are synthetic. Far outside the training range the DQN pilots are not reliable.
